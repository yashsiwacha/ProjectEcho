# Purpose

This document defines how ProjectEcho communicates with users across the application.

It establishes a consistent voice for UI copy, AI-generated insights, notifications, onboarding, and support interactions.

The objective is to build trust, clarity, and confidence through every piece of written communication.

---

# Communication Philosophy

ProjectEcho should communicate like an experienced career mentor:

- Knowledgeable
- Honest
- Encouraging
- Professional
- Transparent
- Practical

The product should help users make informed decisions rather than simply providing answers.

---

# Brand Voice

ProjectEcho is:

- Clear
- Calm
- Intelligent
- Respectful
- Supportive
- Evidence-driven
- Objective

ProjectEcho is never:

- Arrogant
- Overly casual
- Condescending
- Clickbait
- Alarmist
- Manipulative

---

# Communication Principles

## Be Clear

Use plain language.

Avoid unnecessary technical jargon unless the user is expected to understand it.

---

## Be Honest

Never exaggerate confidence.

If AI is uncertain, communicate uncertainty explicitly.

Example:

"Based on your current evidence, this competency appears to be developing."

instead of

"You are highly skilled."

---

## Explain Decisions

Every recommendation should answer:

- Why?
- Based on what?
- What should I do next?

---

## Encourage Progress

Celebrate improvement without exaggeration.

Good:

"You've strengthened your backend competencies with two new projects."

Avoid:

"You're amazing!"

---

## Focus on Action

Whenever possible, conclude with a practical next step.

Example:

"Consider adding deployment evidence to strengthen this competency."

---

# AI Communication

AI explanations should always include:

- Observation
- Supporting Evidence
- Confidence
- Recommendation

Example:

Observation:
Your REST API competency has improved.

Evidence:
Two backend projects demonstrate production-ready REST APIs.

Confidence:
High

Recommendation:
Strengthen authentication and testing to reach the next proficiency level.

---

# Success Messages

Examples:

Evidence uploaded successfully.

Career Passport updated.

Competency recalculated.

Recommendation completed.

Learning progress saved.

---

# Error Messages

Errors should:

- Explain the problem
- Avoid blame
- Offer recovery

Good:

"We couldn't analyze this evidence right now. Please try again."

Avoid:

"Unknown Error."

---

# Empty States

Every empty state should educate users.

Example:

"No evidence has been added yet.

Upload a project, certification, or work experience to begin building your Career Passport."

---

# Notifications

Notifications should be:

- Short
- Actionable
- Informative

Examples:

New recommendation available.

Competency score updated.

Learning milestone completed.

Career goal reached.

---

# Recommendation Language

Recommendations should:

Explain:

- Why it matters
- Expected impact
- Estimated effort

Example:

"Improving PostgreSQL indexing can strengthen your backend competency and increase your Career Readiness score."

---

# Confidence Language

Suggested wording:

Very High

High

Moderate

Low

Insufficient Evidence

Never display percentages without additional explanation.

---

# Accessibility

Content should:

- Use simple language
- Avoid ambiguous wording
- Be understandable for non-native English speakers
- Minimize unnecessary abbreviations

---

# Inclusive Language

Use:

"They"

"The user"

"Your profile"

Avoid assumptions regarding:

- Gender
- Experience
- Education
- Career path

---

# Writing Style

Prefer:

Short sentences

Active voice

Concrete recommendations

Evidence-based statements

Avoid:

Marketing buzzwords

Corporate jargon

Excessive enthusiasm

---

# Governance

All user-facing content should follow this guide.

Any new AI prompt, notification, onboarding flow, or interface copy should be reviewed against these principles before release.
